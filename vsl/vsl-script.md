**VO (1:25–2:40):**
> In the last two years, writing got faster than editing. So naturally, everybody stopped editing. And what's left is a draft with no evidence, no rhythm, and no position — three failures, on three different layers.
>
> Which is why "make it sound more human" never works. You're swapping words on the wrong layer.
>
> So I built ProofMark — the Human Edit System. It's one mechanism: E·S·D.
>
> **E — Evidence.** Three verifiable specifics per section. Numbers, names, dates, things that actually happened. This turns opinion into information.
>
> **S — Structure.** Break the even hum on purpose. Short landings. Long builds. Uneven lists. This is what a human ear actually recognises.
>
> **D — Distinction.** One position you'd defend in an argument, unhedged. Nothing signals authorship like being willing to be wrong.
>
> Run them in that order, and the "is this AI?" question stops being interesting. Because now the reader has a *reason* to keep reading.
